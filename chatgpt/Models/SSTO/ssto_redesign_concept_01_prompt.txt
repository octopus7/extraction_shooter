Design the silhouette first: a broad, deep-bodied lifting body, shaped like a clipped kite seen from above, with a short softly chined prow, a full-height midbody and shoulders that flow continuously into swept and cropped wings. Low-profile, almost tailless, with a pair of short outward-canted tips at the stern, no tall upright tails. Broad enough to read as a working spacefaring shuttle with a standing-height walk-through cargo cabin, not a fighter; roughly the scale of a small regional aircraft. The bridge is a slender faceted band of petrol-green smoked glass inset into the brow above a blunt dark chin. The belly is deep and heat-tiled in graphite, wrapping up in a single clean chine around the nose. The forward fuselage, wing roots and shoulders share continuous sculpted curvature and purposeful, sensibly large ceramic panels. Two large dark exhaust bells are recessed side by side into the width of the stern between a pair of protective shrouds; the engines are built into the hull. A small inset door on the port side behind the bridge has a short fold-down boarding stair; stout landing struts and broad feet designed for a flat field. Flush underside lift bay doors are a subtle feature. Practical joints, recessed latches and slight heat discoloration at the back. Hull chiefly warm bone-white / pale mineral gray, graphite lower third, one distinctive broad, asymmetric burnt-sienna service band curving up from the port hatch and over the shoulder, tiny amber service lights. Show the hatch, glass and warp of the shoulder catching light, and the contrast between broad smooth surfaces, fine tile seams and darker panel recesses. Striking and believable at a distance, tactile up close. No weapons, military symbols, boosters, detached modules, long thin nose, chunky rectangular body with wings tacked on, exaggerated vents or scattered greeble.
